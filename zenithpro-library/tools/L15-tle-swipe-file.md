# The TLE Swipe File: Copy-Paste Prompt Blocks

**Methods cage. Context arms. Outcomes hunt.**

Stop writing prompts from scratch. Copy a T-block, an E-block, and an L-block. Paste them together. You just built a Mission Order.

### How to Use This File

1. **Find your use case** below (emails, social posts, sales pages, etc.)
2. **Copy a T-Block** — Force the AI to read, think, and adopt context (be strict here)
3. **Copy an E-Block** — Describe the feeling, impact, or result you want (be visionary here)
4. **Copy an L-Block** — Release control on format, length, and structure (let go here)
5. **Paste them together** and fill in any [BRACKETS] with your specifics
6. **Send it** — the AI now has context, a destination, and creative freedom

### Quick Reference

| Role | What You Control | Example |
|------|-----------------|---------|
| **T - TYRANT** | Input: what AI reads, knows, becomes | "Read every word of this file. Adopt this persona." |
| **L - LIBERTARIAN** | Output: release format, length, structure | "Use whatever structure hits hardest." |
| **E - EVANGELIST** | Outcome: the feeling, result, transformation | "The reader should feel seen, not sold." |

## 1. Marketing Emails (Newsletters, Promos, Sequences)

**[T - TYRANT: Arm the Context]**
- "Read the attached customer survey results. Use ONLY language the customers actually used — do not add marketing-speak."
- "Here is everything about this offer: [paste offer details]. You must understand the full offer before writing a single word."
- "Adopt the voice of [YOUR NAME] — conversational, direct, no corporate fluff. Read these 3 example emails for voice calibration: [paste examples]."
- "The reader has been on my list for 6+ months. They've seen dozens of my emails. Do not write like this is our first interaction."
- "Reference the specific pain point from last week's email: [paste the line]. This email is a direct follow-up to that thread."
- "This is email 4 of 7 in a launch sequence. Read the prior 3 emails [paste them] so you understand what's already been said and don't repeat it."
- "The subscriber clicked on a link about [TOPIC]. That click is the only reason they're getting this email. Every sentence must connect to that specific interest."
- "My audience is [AUDIENCE DESCRIPTION]. They are skeptical of hype, allergic to buzzwords, and have seen every marketing trick. Write for people who are smart and busy."
- "Here is the testimonial from [CLIENT NAME]: [paste it]. Build the email around this real result — not a hypothetical."
- "Do not use these words or phrases under any circumstances: 'unlock,' 'game-changer,' 'level up,' 'deep dive,' 'leverage.' These are dead to me."

**[E - EVANGELIST: Aim the Outcome]**
- "The reader should feel like I wrote this email specifically for them at 11pm because I couldn't stop thinking about their situation."
- "By the end, they should feel foolish for NOT clicking the link. Not pressured — foolish. Like they'd be leaving money on the table."
- "This email should make the reader think: 'Finally, someone who actually gets what I'm dealing with.'"
- "The subject line should create a curiosity gap so strong that NOT opening the email feels like a loss."
- "I want the reader to forward this to a friend and say: 'You need to read this. This is exactly what we were talking about.'"
- "The tone should feel like a trusted advisor pulling someone aside at a conference — not a sales pitch."
- "The reader should finish this email believing something different than when they started. Not just informed — shifted."
- "Make the offer feel inevitable. By the time they see the price, they should already be reaching for their wallet."
- "The unsubscribe rate on this email should be zero because even people who don't buy feel like they got value."
- "If someone reads only the first two sentences and the P.S., they should still understand the core message and feel compelled to act."

**[L - LIBERTARIAN: Free the Format]**
- "I don't care about email length. Make it as long as it needs to be and not one word longer."
- "Format this however gets the point across fastest. One long paragraph, bullet points, numbered list — your call."
- "You choose the hook. You choose the structure. You choose where the CTA goes. I only care that it works."
- "Don't follow an email template. Write the email that THIS message needs to be."
- "Use line breaks, bold, italics — whatever creates the reading rhythm that pulls them through."

## 2. Social Media Posts (LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Instagram)

**[T - TYRANT: Arm the Context]**
- "Read the attached article/transcript. Use ONLY ideas from this source. Do not add outside concepts or generic wisdom."
- "Adopt the persona of someone who has built and sold 3 businesses and is genuinely frustrated with the advice they see online."
- "My audience is [DESCRIPTION]. They are NOT beginners. Do not explain basics. Write for people who are already in the game."
- "Reference this specific data point: [paste it]. Build the post around this single proof point."
- "Read these 5 posts that performed well for me: [paste them]. Match the voice, rhythm, and energy — not the content."
- "This post is a response to [TRENDING TOPIC/POST]. Read it first: [paste or link]. My take is contrarian to the consensus."
- "I am [YOUR NAME], [YOUR ROLE]. Here are 3 things I believe that most people in my industry would disagree with: [list them]. These beliefs should come through in the post."
- "Do not use hashtags. Do not use emojis. Do not start with 'I.' Do not ask a question at the end just to drive engagement."
- "The post must be based on something that actually happened to me or my clients — not a hypothetical. Here's the real story: [paste it]."
- "Read my bio: [paste it]. The post should make someone who reads it immediately want to click through to learn more about me."

**[E - EVANGELIST: Aim the Outcome]**
- "The reader should stop scrolling because they feel personally called out — like I'm describing their exact situation."
- "50% of readers should strongly agree. 50% should feel uncomfortable. I want polarization, not applause."
- "The reader should feel like they've been doing it wrong their whole career — and that the fix is simpler than they thought."
- "This should feel like overhearing a conversation you weren't supposed to hear — raw, honest, slightly uncomfortable."
- "Someone should screenshot this post and text it to a friend with the message: 'THIS.'"
- "The first line should hit so hard that even people who disagree can't scroll past without reading the rest."
- "Make the reader feel smart for agreeing with me. Like they've been thinking this but couldn't articulate it."
- "By the end, the reader should have a new mental model they didn't have before. Not just information — a lens."
- "The post should feel like a late-night conversation between two people who actually know what they're talking about."
- "I want the comment section to be a war zone. The post should be a grenade, not a greeting card."

**[L - LIBERTARIAN: Free the Format]**
- "Use whatever sentence structure creates the most punch. I don't care about grammar rules if breaking them hits harder."
- "Length doesn't matter. Make it as short as possible while still being devastating."
- "Format this however you think will stop the scroll. One-line paragraphs, long blocks, a single sentence — your call."
- "You choose whether this is a story, a rant, a listicle, a confession, or something else entirely."
- "Don't follow any social media post template. Write the thing that would make ME want to post it."

## 3. Sales Pages & Offers

**[T - TYRANT: Arm the Context]**
- "Here is everything about this offer: [paste full offer details — price, bonuses, guarantee, deliverables, timeline]. You must reference all components accurately."
- "Read these 5 customer testimonials: [paste them]. Use their EXACT words and phrases — not your interpretation of what they said."
- "My ideal buyer is: [paste avatar description]. Write exclusively for this person. Everyone else is invisible."
- "The main objection is: '[SPECIFIC OBJECTION].' This page must neutralize it by the halfway point."
- "Here is the competitor's page: [paste or describe]. My offer must feel categorically different — not incrementally better."
- "The price is $[AMOUNT]. Do not apologize for it, justify it through value, or use the phrase 'invest in yourself.' Present it as the obvious choice."
- "Read this case study: [paste it]. This transformation — from [BEFORE] to [AFTER] — is the proof the page is built around."
- "Do not use these words: 'revolutionary,' 'game-changing,' 'transformative,' 'unlock your potential,' 'journey.' My audience has been burned by these."
- "The guarantee is: [EXACT GUARANTEE TERMS]. It must be the most visible element on the page after the headline."
- "This offer closes on [DATE]. The urgency is real, not manufactured. Reference the actual deadline and what happens after it."

**[E - EVANGELIST: Aim the Outcome]**
- "The prospect should feel stupid for saying no. Not pressured — stupid. Like turning down a deal they'll regret."
- "The perceived value should feel 10x the price before they ever see the price."
- "Make the pain of staying the same feel unbearable compared to the cost of buying."
- "The reader should feel like I understand their problem better than they understand it themselves."
- "By the guarantee section, any remaining risk should feel laughable. They should think: 'Why WOULDN'T I try this?'"
- "The headline should make someone who wasn't planning to buy stop and read the full page."
- "The testimonials should make the reader think: 'That person is just like me. If it worked for them...'"
- "The reader should feel increasingly uncomfortable about their current situation with every section they read."
- "After reading the bonuses, the reader should feel like the bonuses alone are worth the price."
- "The close should feel like a friend saying 'seriously, just do it' — not a salesperson applying pressure."

**[L - LIBERTARIAN: Free the Format]**
- "Structure the page in whatever order creates the strongest emotional arc. Don't follow a template — follow the psychology."
- "You decide where the price reveal goes. You decide how many sections. You decide the flow."
- "Use long copy or short copy — whatever this offer needs. I care about conversion, not word count."
- "Format the guarantee however makes it most visible and believable."
- "You choose the headline style. Curiosity, bold claim, question, story — whatever stops them."

## 4. Blog Posts & Articles

**[T - TYRANT: Arm the Context]**
- "Read the attached research/source material: [paste or path]. Every claim in this article must trace back to this source. No hallucinated statistics."
- "My audience is: [DESCRIPTION]. They read [PUBLICATIONS THEY READ]. Match that sophistication level — don't write down to them."
- "Target keyword is '[KEYWORD].' It must appear in the title, first 100 words, one H2, and the conclusion. Do not stuff it elsewhere."
- "Here are 3 articles that already rank for this keyword: [paste URLs or content]. My article must offer something none of them cover."
- "Adopt my voice. Read these 3 published pieces for calibration: [paste excerpts]. Match the tone, not the content."
- "This article is a follow-up to: [paste previous article]. Reference it explicitly and build on its conclusions."
- "Do not use stock phrases: 'in today's fast-paced world,' 'at the end of the day,' 'it goes without saying,' 'the bottom line is.' These are dead."
- "Include this specific example/case study: [paste it]. It's the centerpiece — the article exists to frame and deliver this proof."
- "The reader is someone who has tried [COMMON APPROACH] and failed. They don't need to be convinced the problem exists — they need a new solution."
- "I have a contrarian position on this topic: [STATE IT]. The article should present this position unapologetically with evidence."

**[E - EVANGELIST: Aim the Outcome]**
- "The reader should finish this article and feel like they have a new mental model — not just new information."
- "Someone should read this and immediately share it with a colleague saying: 'This changes how I think about [TOPIC].'"
- "The opening should create an itch the reader can't scratch without reading the full article."
- "By the midpoint, the reader should feel like their previous approach was backwards — and that the fix is obvious once you see it."
- "The article should make me the person they think of whenever [TOPIC] comes up."
- "A reader should be able to explain the core idea to someone else in one sentence after reading this."
- "The conclusion should feel like a door opening, not a door closing. They should want to go DO something."
- "This article should rank #1 AND be the one people actually read and share. Not just SEO-optimized — genuinely useful."

**[L - LIBERTARIAN: Free the Format]**
- "Structure this however makes the argument most compelling. Listicle, narrative, inverted pyramid — your call."
- "Length is whatever the topic demands. Don't pad it and don't cut it short."
- "Use subheadings, pull quotes, bold text — whatever makes this scannable for busy people AND rewarding for deep readers."
- "You choose the opening style. Anecdote, data point, provocative claim, question — whatever hooks hardest."

**[T - TYRANT: Arm the Context]**

- "Adopt my speaking voice. Read this transcript of me talking naturally: [paste excerpt]. Match how I actually talk, not how a scriptwriter thinks I should talk."
- "The video is [X] minutes max. Every second must earn its place. If a sentence doesn't advance the argument or create tension, cut it."
- "This is a YouTube video competing for attention against [COMPETITOR VIDEOS ON THIS TOPIC]. My angle is: [YOUR ANGLE]."

**[E - EVANGELIST: Aim the Outcome]**
- "In the first 8 seconds, the viewer should think: 'This person knows a secret I need.' They should feel FOMO for not knowing it yet."
- "The viewer should feel an open loop of curiosity so strong that clicking away feels like a loss."
- "The pacing should feel breathless during the problem setup and calm during the solution — like the relief after a storm."
- "By the end, the viewer should feel equipped, not just informed. They should be ready to go DO the thing."
- "The viewer should feel like they just had a private conversation with someone who actually knows what they're talking about."
- "Mid-video, the viewer should think: 'Why has nobody explained it this way before?'"
- "The CTA should feel earned — like a natural next step — not a sales pitch bolted onto the end."
- "Someone should watch this video and immediately text the link to a friend who's struggling with [TOPIC]."
- "The thumbnail/title combo should make someone who sees it in their feed unable to NOT click."

**[L - LIBERTARIAN: Free the Format]**
- "Write the script however it flows best. Don't follow a rigid intro-body-outro template if the content wants to move differently."
- "You choose where to break for B-roll callouts, visual changes, or emphasis. Mark them naturally."
- "Don't worry about standard script formatting. Make the flow work for speaking out loud."
- "You decide the pacing. Speed up, slow down, pause for emphasis — script it how it should FEEL."

## Bonus: Universal T-Blocks (Use Anywhere)

These work in ANY context. Paste them at the top of any prompt.

- "Read the attached file completely before responding. Do not skim. Do not summarize prematurely."
- "Adopt this persona: [DESCRIPTION]. Stay in character for the entire response."
- "Here is my voice/brand guide: [paste key points]. Match my voice — not a generic professional voice."
- "Do not hallucinate facts, statistics, quotes, or sources. If you're not sure, say so."
- "Before writing, identify the single most important thing the reader/viewer needs to walk away with. Build everything around that."
- "Assume I am an expert in this field. Do not explain basics. Do not qualify obvious statements."
- "Here is what I do NOT want: [LIST SPECIFIC THINGS]. These are hard constraints, not suggestions."
- "Read these examples of what 'good' looks like for this task: [paste 2-3 examples]. Calibrate your quality bar to these."

## Bonus: Universal E-Blocks (Use Anywhere)

These work in ANY context. Paste them to set the destination.

- "The reader should feel like this was written specifically for them — not for 'an audience.'"
- "Someone should read this and immediately want to share it with the note: 'You need to see this.'"
- "The first sentence should make it impossible to stop reading."
- "Make me sound like the smartest person in the room who also happens to be the most approachable."
- "The reader should understand the core idea well enough to explain it to someone else in 30 seconds."
- "This should feel like a conversation, not a document."
- "By the end, the reader should feel compelled to act — not just informed."
- "The tone should be: confident without being arrogant, specific without being dry, urgent without being pushy."

## Bonus: Universal L-Blocks (Use Anywhere)

These work in ANY context. Paste them to release the leash.

- "Use whatever structure serves the message best. Don't follow a template."
- "Length is irrelevant. Clarity and impact are everything."
- "You choose the format. I'll judge the result, not the method."
- "Break any writing convention if breaking it makes the piece stronger."
- "Don't write what you think I want to hear. Write what actually works."
- "Surprise me. I'd rather see something unexpected that hits hard than something safe that doesn't."
