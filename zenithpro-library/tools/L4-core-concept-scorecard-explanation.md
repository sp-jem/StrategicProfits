## WHY THIS SCORECARD EXISTS

### The Mark Ford Moment That Changed Everything

In 2004, Rich Schefren attended a strategy summit at Jekyll Island with Jay Abraham and other marketing legends. They were brainstorming ideas to follow up the wildly successful "End of America" promotion that had generated $500 million in sales.

After a full day of idea generation, Rich walked with his mentor Mark Ford and asked, "How do you think it's going?"

Mark's response changed how Rich thought about marketing forever:

> "Everyone in the room thinks it's just about the idea. It's not just about the idea. It's about the articulation of the idea, because it has to be articulated in a way that it actually hits, that people remember, that they can repeat. Change just a couple words in that articulation, and the thing could bomb."

This scorecard operationalizes that principle. But it goes further.

**Because there's a second, even more critical test that most marketers never consider:**

Even if you have a brilliantly articulated Core Concept that everyone remembers and repeats... **does it drive YOUR business, or does it make your competitors rich?**

That's what this scorecard is designed to catch.

## THE TWO FATAL MISTAKES THIS PREVENTS

### Fatal Mistake #1: Building a Belief Chain to the Wrong Destination

Imagine spending 20+ hours building a complete belief chain—mapping dependencies, creating content for each belief, sequencing everything perfectly—only to discover that when prospects fully believe your Core Concept, they:

- Hire a different type of solution provider
- Try to DIY it
- Buy from your competitor
- Solve the problem a completely different way

**This happens when you skip validation.**

You have a great Core Concept. Great articulation. But the logical chain from "I believe the CC" to "I must buy from YOU specifically" has a gap you never noticed.

The belief chain installs the belief perfectly. You've done brilliant work. And then prospects thank you and go somewhere else.

**This scorecard prevents that.**

### Fatal Mistake #2: Clever Wordplay That Doesn't Create Demand

The second fatal mistake is creating a Core Concept that:

- Sounds brilliant
- Is memorable and quotable
- Gets shared on social media
- Makes you look smart
- Wins awards at marketing conferences

**And doesn't generate a single dollar of revenue.**

Why? Because it doesn't actually create demand. It's clever, not commercial.

It might explain the market. It might be true. It might be insightful.

But when someone believes it, they don't feel compelled to buy anything.

**This scorecard catches that too.**

In traditional marketing, you:

1. Come up with a Core Concept

3. Build all your marketing around it

In ZenithPro's D3 methodology, you:

**The difference:** You validate the destination before building the path.

Think about it: Would you rather spend 3 hours validating and refining your Core Concept, or spend 20 hours building a belief chain that leads to a competitor's solution?

## HOW THE SCORECARD WORKS

### The 12-Category System

We evaluate your Core Concept across 12 distinct dimensions, each weighted by its importance to business success.

**The categories are:**

1. **Articulation Simplicity** (10%)
2. **Memorability & Repeatability** (10%)
3. **Image Strength** (8%)
4. **USP Connection Logic** (12%) ← **HIGHEST WEIGHT**
5. **Category Creation Power** (10%)
6. **Competitive Differentiation** (8%)
7. **Past Failure Explanation** (9%)
8. **Current Belief Compatibility** (7%)
9. **Business Growth Driver** (10%)
10. **Emotional Resonance** (8%)
11. **Market Sophistication Alignment** (4%)
12. **Viral Sharability** (4%)

**Total: 100 points**

Each category contains 3-6 specific sub-questions rated on a 1-5 scale, then calculated to contribute to the weighted total.

**Your final score: 0-100**

- **90-100**: Exceptional - build the chain now
- **80-89**: Strong - proceed with minor notes
- **70-79**: Needs work - refine before building
- **60-69**: Weak - fundamental rework required
- **Below 60**: Start over - wrong Core Concept

## THE 12 CATEGORIES EXPLAINED

### CATEGORY 1: ARTICULATION SIMPLICITY (10%)

**What We're Testing:** Can your Core Concept be stated in one clear sentence that a 5th grader would understand?

**Why It Matters:** Complex Core Concepts don't spread. If prospects can't grasp it immediately, they won't remember it, repeat it, or act on it.

**The Mark Ford Principle in Action:**

Same idea, different articulations:

❌ **Version A:** "The efficacy of tactical marketing implementation is contingent upon the establishment of strategic market positioning as a prerequisite foundation."

✅ **Version B:** "Positioning trumps tactics."

One gets repeated. One gets forgotten.

**What We're Scoring:**

- Can it be stated in ONE sentence? (Not a paragraph)
- Does it use concrete words vs. abstractions?
- Could a child understand it?
- Can you explain it in under 15 seconds?
- Does it require teaching new concepts first?

**High Score Example:** "Your ads aren't broken. Your position's unspoken."

- One sentence (actually two short ones)
- All concrete words
- Instantly clear
- No new concepts needed
- **Score: 9-10/10**

**Low Score Example:** "Marketing ROI optimization requires strategic synergistic alignment of multi-channel attribution models with stakeholder value propositions."

- Too many words
- All abstract concepts
- Requires translation
- Confusing
- **Score: 2-3/10**

---

### CATEGORY 2: MEMORABILITY & REPEATABILITY (10%)

**What We're Testing:** Can someone hear it once and repeat it exactly? Will they WANT to share it?

**Why It Matters:** Your Core Concept doesn't install itself. It spreads through repetition. If prospects can't remember it word-for-word, you're fighting an uphill battle.

**The Social Capital Test:** Does sharing this make the sharer look smart, or make them look confused?

**What We're Scoring:**

- Can someone repeat it verbatim after one hearing?
- Does it use memorable linguistic devices (rhyme, rhythm, alliteration)?
- Is it "sticky" - lodges in your mind?
- Does sharing it increase social status?
- Would they naturally reference it in conversation?

**The 24-Hour Test:**

Tell someone your Core Concept. Check back 24 hours later. Can they repeat it?

- **Verbatim** = 5 points
- **Close paraphrase** = 3-4 points
- **General idea** = 2 points
- **Can't recall** = 1 point

**High Score Examples:**

"Your ads aren't broken. Your position's unspoken."

- Rhyme creates instant memory hook
- Parallel structure (broken/unspoken)
- Rhythmic cadence
- **Score: 9-10/10**

"If she hits on you first, you're golden. If you hit on her first, you're done."

- Clear contrast
- Memorable pattern
- Easy to repeat
- **Score: 9/10**

**Low Score Example:**

"Success in modern marketing requires understanding the intersection between strategic positioning and tactical execution within the context of your unique value proposition."

- No memory hooks
- Too long
- No pattern
- **Score: 2/10**

### CATEGORY 3: IMAGE STRENGTH (8%)

**What We're Testing:** Does it create a vivid mental picture, or is it pure abstraction?

**Why It Matters:** Humans think in images. Abstract concepts require mental translation. Images stick immediately.

**The Neuroscience:** Your brain processes images 60,000 times faster than text. If your Core Concept creates a picture, it installs faster and lasts longer.

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does it evoke a strong visual image?
- Can you draw/diagram it?
- Does it use sensory/physical language?
- Does it show before/after imagery?

**High Score Examples:**

"Your penile arteries are clogged. Clear the obstruction, blood flows freely."

- Instant visual: clogged pipes
- Physical/tangible
- Clear before/after
- **Score: 9/10**

"Your ads aren't broken. Your position's unspoken."

- Visual metaphor: broken machine
- Can imagine both states
- Physical language
- **Score: 7-8/8**

**Low Score Example:**

"Optimize synergistic value delivery through stakeholder alignment."

- No image forms
- Pure abstraction
- Can't visualize
- **Score: 2/8**

**Pro Tip:** If you can't draw your Core Concept on a napkin, it probably scores low here.

### CATEGORY 4: USP CONNECTION LOGIC (12%) ⭐ HIGHEST WEIGHT

**What We're Testing:** If someone fully believes your Core Concept, must they logically buy YOUR solution specifically, or could they believe it and buy from someone else?

**Why It Matters:** This is the business-critical test. Everything else is decoration if this fails.

**The Fatal Question:** "What could someone believe my Core Concept and still NOT buy from me?"

If the answer is "lots of things," you have a problem.

**What We're Scoring:**

The logical chain:

1. Prospect accepts Core Concept
2. Core Concept implies a requirement
3. Requirement can only be fulfilled by...
4. YOUR specific USP

**Each link must be airtight.**

**The Three Connection Levels:**

**LEVEL 1: WEAK CONNECTION (Score: 3-5/12)**

CC: "Marketing requires consistent effort" USP: "My course teaches Facebook ads"

Problem: They could believe CC and:

- Hire any marketing agency
- Take any marketing course
- DIY with free resources
- Hire a different type of marketer

**The CC doesn't point to YOU.**

**LEVEL 2: CATEGORY CONNECTION (Score: 6-8/12)**

CC: "You don't have a marketing problem, you have a positioning problem" USP: "I help with positioning strategy"

Problem: They could believe CC and:

- Hire any positioning consultant
- Take a positioning course
- Hire a branding agency

**The CC creates category demand but not provider specificity.**

**LEVEL 3: TIGHT CONNECTION (Score: 10-12/12)**

CC: "You don't have a marketing problem, you have a positioning problem. And positioning without implementation coaching leaves you stuck, while DIY courses overwhelm you."

USP: "I provide done-with-you positioning that combines strategy AND implementation coaching."

Result: The CC makes YOUR approach the logical necessity.

**This is what we're looking for.**

**The Competitive Vulnerability Test:**

After hearing your CC, could prospects logically conclude they should:

- DIY it? (If yes, your CC doesn't necessitate help)
- Hire a different category? (If yes, wrong CC for your USP)
- Choose a competitor? (If yes, CC is too generic)

**Why This Has 12% Weight (The Highest):**

Because you could score 95 on articulation (Categories 1-3) and 90 on emotional resonance (Category 10) and have a Core Concept that:

- Everyone remembers
- Everyone repeats
- Creates excitement
- Drives zero revenue to you

**Example of Fatal Flaw:**

CC: "AI is the future of marketing" (Brilliantly articulated, memorable, emotionally resonant)

USP: "I do Facebook ad management"

Gap: They believe CC → research AI marketing tools → buy software → you get nothing

**Your CC made AI software companies rich.**

This category prevents that.

### CATEGORY 5: CATEGORY CREATION POWER (10%)

**What We're Testing:** Does your Core Concept create a NEW category where you're the obvious leader, or does it just position you better in an existing crowded category?

**Why It Matters:** Categories of one command premium pricing, attract ideal clients, and eliminate direct competition. Better positioning in an existing category means you're still competing on the same dimensions as everyone else.

**The "Going Back" Test:**

After someone truly understands your Core Concept, can they go back to thinking about the problem the old way?

- If NO → category created
- If YES → better positioning only

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does the CC reveal a completely NEW way to think about the problem?
- After hearing it, does the old category feel incomplete/wrong?
- Does it make existing solutions suddenly seem irrelevant?
- Can you NAME the new category this creates?
- Would competitors struggle to credibly claim this category?

**High Score Example:**

CC: "You don't have a marketing problem, you have a positioning problem" New Category: "Strategic Positioning" (vs generic "Marketing Consulting")

Effect:

- Old category: Marketing help (tactics, execution, campaigns)
- New category: Positioning strategy (before tactics even matter)
- Existing solutions: Feel incomplete—they're solving wrong problem

**Score: 9/10**

**Medium Score Example:**

CC: "Email marketing done right gets better results than social media" Category: Still "Email Marketing Services"

Effect:

- Makes a claim WITHIN existing category
- Better positioning, but same competitive set
- Still comparing to other email marketers

**Score: 5/10**

**The Category Leader Premium:**

When you create a category:

- You define the rules
- Competitors look like followers
- Premium pricing is justified
- Ideal clients self-select

When you compete in existing category:

- Rules are set
- Price competition
- Undifferentiated in prospect's mind

### CATEGORY 6: COMPETITIVE DIFFERENTIATION (8%)

**What We're Testing:** Does the Core Concept inherently make competitor solutions seem wrong, or do you have to explicitly bash competitors separately?

**Why It Matters:** The best Core Concepts automatically destroy other solutions by their very logic. You don't need to name competitors or do explicit "us vs. them" comparisons.

**The Elegant Destruction Principle:**

If someone accepts your CC, competing approaches should feel obviously flawed—without you ever mentioning them.

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does the CC inherently explain why popular solutions fail?
- Does accepting the CC force rejection of competitor approaches?
- Does it reframe the market so you're the obvious choice?
- Could a competitor use this CC against YOU?

**High Score Example:**

CC: "Positioning without demand creation is just expensive branding"

Automatic destructions:

- Branding agencies → just spending money on awareness
- Generic positioning consultants → don't create demand
- DIY positioning → you're just differentiating, not creating pull

You never named them, but the CC makes their approaches seem incomplete.

**Score: 8/8**

**Low Score Example:**

CC: "Good marketing requires testing"

Problem:

- Every competitor also does testing
- Doesn't differentiate at all
- Could be their tagline too

**Score: 2/8**

**The Reverse Test:**

Could your competitor steal your Core Concept and use it effectively?

- If YES → it's too generic (low score)
- If NO → it's specific to your methodology (high score)

### CATEGORY 7: PAST FAILURE EXPLANATION (9%)

**What We're Testing:** Does your Core Concept explain why prospects failed before WITHOUT making them feel stupid?

**Why It Matters:** Rich's principle: "Make their beliefs feel incomplete, not wrong." If your CC explains past failures while removing personal blame, prospects feel relief and hope instead of shame.

**The "Incomplete Rather Than Wrong" Framework:**

❌ **Wrong Approach:** "You failed because you did it wrong"

- Result: Shame, resistance, defensiveness

✅ **Right Approach:** "You had 90% of the truth. Here's the 10% you were missing"

- Result: Relief, hope, openness

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does the CC explain why past solutions failed them?
- Does it remove personal blame for failure?
- Does it make old beliefs feel incomplete (not wrong)?
- Does it create "THAT'S why!" epiphany moments?
- Does it explain failures across ALL solution types they tried?

**High Score Example:**

CC: "Marketing tactics fail when positioning is missing—not because you executed poorly, but because no amount of execution fixes wrong positioning"

Effect on past failures:

- Facebook ads didn't work → "It wasn't the ads, it was positioning"
- Funnel didn't convert → "It wasn't the funnel, it was positioning"
- Email campaigns flopped → "It wasn't the emails, it was positioning"

**Universal explanation. Zero blame. Total relief.**

Score: 9/9

**Low Score Example:**

CC: "You need to work harder and be more consistent"

Effect:

- Implies they were lazy/inconsistent
- Creates shame
- No relief or hope

**Score: 2/9**

**The Relief Test:**

When you share your CC, do prospects visibly relax and say:

- "Oh, so it WASN'T my fault"
- "That explains EVERYTHING"
- "I knew something was off but couldn't name it"

If YES → high score If NO → low score

### CATEGORY 8: CURRENT BELIEF COMPATIBILITY (7%)

**What We're Testing:** Does your Core Concept work WITH existing beliefs, or does it require prospects to abandon identity-level worldviews?

**Why It Matters:** Changing surface-level beliefs is easy. Changing identity-level beliefs is nearly impossible. The best Core Concepts leverage existing worldviews rather than fighting them.

**The Identity Proximity Rule:**

The closer a belief is to someone's identity, the harder it is to change.

Surface belief: "Facebook ads don't work for me" → Easy to change Core belief: "I'm not good at marketing" → Medium difficulty Identity belief: "I'm not a business person" → Nearly impossible

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does it leverage what they already believe vs. requiring belief change?
- Does it give them permission to admit what they already suspect?
- Does it align with their identity and self-image?
- Would accepting this make them feel SMARTER?

**High Score Example:**

CC: "It's not that ads don't work for small businesses. Big companies have positioning, small businesses have hustle. Once you add positioning to your hustle, everything changes."

Effect:

- Leverages existing belief: "I work hard"
- Validates their effort: "Your hustle is valuable"
- Reframes failure: "You just needed positioning"
- Makes them feel smart: "I already had half the equation"

**Score: 7/7**

**Low Score Example:**

CC: "You need to completely change your personality and become more aggressive"

Effect:

- Attacks identity
- Requires fundamental change
- Creates resistance
- Makes them feel inadequate

**Score: 1/7**

**The Permission Principle:**

Great Core Concepts give permission to believe what they already suspected but didn't feel allowed to say.

"I knew tactics weren't enough, but everyone kept selling me tactics."

### CATEGORY 9: BUSINESS GROWTH DRIVER (10%)

**What We're Testing:** Does this Core Concept actually drive revenue growth, or does it just sound clever in theory?

**Why It Matters:** This is the ultimate business reality check. Marketing that doesn't make money is a hobby.

**The Four Business Tests:**

**Test 1: Demand Creation vs. Demand Capture**

Does your CC:

- Create NEW demand in the 97% not actively shopping?
- Or just capture existing demand from the 3% already looking?

**Test 2: Premium Pricing**

Does your CC:

- Justify 2-3x market rates because of category-of-one positioning?
- Or force competitive pricing because you're in crowded category?

**Test 3: Sales Cycle**

Does your CC:

- Make the decision obvious quickly?
- Or require extensive additional persuasion?

**Test 4: Client Quality**

Does your CC:

- Attract ideal clients and repel poor fits?
- Or attract everyone (including wrong people who won't succeed)?

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does the CC create DEMAND or just capture existing demand?
- Does it command premium pricing?
- Does it shorten sales cycles?
- Does it attract ideal clients and repel poor fits?
- Can you scale it across avatars/markets?

**High Score Example:**

CC: "You don't have a marketing problem, you have a positioning problem"

Business Effects:

- Creates NEW demand: People not shopping for help suddenly realize they need it
- Premium pricing: Strategic positioning commands higher rates than "marketing help"
- Short cycle: Once they see positioning gap, urgency is high
- Pre-qualification: Attracts established businesses, repels startup tire-kickers

**Score: 9/10**

**Low Score Example:**

CC: "Get more website traffic"

Business Effects:

- Captures existing demand: Only people already wanting traffic respond
- Price compression: Competing with every SEO/traffic provider
- Long cycle: Have to convince them traffic matters
- Wrong clients: Attracts people who think traffic = success

**Score: 3/10**

**The Honest Question:**

If you perfectly install this belief in 100 prospects, how many:

- Take next step immediately?
- Ask about pricing?
- Request to start?
- Tell others about it?

If the answer is "most of them" → high score If the answer is "a few" → low score

**Why It Matters:** Logic opens the mind, emotion opens the wallet. Great Core Concepts leverage logic but trigger emotional response.

When you share your Core Concept in person, do you see:

- Nodding
- Excitement
- "Tell me more" energy

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does it make prospects FEEL something?
- When you share it, do people's eyes light up?
- Does it tap into hope or eliminate fear?
- Does it create "I knew it!" satisfaction?

**High Score Example:**

CC: "If SHE hits on YOU first, 90% of the time she's into you. If YOU hit on HER first, 90% of the time she thinks you're desperate."

Emotional Effect:

- Relief: "No wonder approaching hasn't worked"
- Hope: "There's a better way"
- Excitement: "I want that reality"
- Validation: "I always felt that but didn't have permission to say it"

**Score: 8/8**

**Low Score Example:**

CC: "Optimize conversion rate through multivariate testing"

Emotional Effect:

- None
- Intellectual
- Boring
- No feeling

**Score: 2/8**

**The Visceral Test:**

Does your CC trigger:

- ✅ Relief (fear eliminated)
- ✅ Hope (possibility opened)
- ✅ Excitement (energy activated)
- ✅ Validation (suspicion confirmed)
- ✅ Anger (at system that hid this)

Or does it trigger:

- ❌ Confusion
- ❌ Indifference
- ❌ Skepticism without curiosity

### CATEGORY 11: MARKET SOPHISTICATION ALIGNMENT (4%)

**What We're Testing:** Is your Core Concept's complexity level right for your market's sophistication, or is it too simple/complex?

**Why It Matters:** Eugene Schwartz's awareness stages matter. A Level 5 sophisticated market needs NEW angles. A Level 1 market needs SIMPLE entry points.

**The Sophistication Mismatch Problem:**

- Sophisticated market + simple CC = "I've heard this before"
- Unsophisticated market + complex CC = "I'm confused"

**What We're Scoring:**

- Does complexity match market sophistication?
- Does it address the right awareness stage?
- For sophisticated markets: Is it NEW enough?
- For unsophisticated markets: Is it SIMPLE enough?

**This is only 4% because:**

IF you've nailed the other categories, sophistication alignment will likely be fine. This is a fine-tuning category, not a foundational one.

### CATEGORY 12: VIRAL SHARABILITY (4%)

**What We're Testing:** Will your Core Concept spread organically through conversation and social media, reducing your customer acquisition cost?

**Why It Matters:** The more it spreads on its own, the less you pay for awareness. But virality without business logic (Category 4, 9) is useless.

**The Water Cooler Test:**

Would someone naturally share this at:

- Cocktail party
- Business lunch
- Social media
- Group chat
- Conference

**What We're Scoring:**

- Is it inherently shareable?
- Does it tap into cultural zeitgeist or timely trends?
- Is it quotable/screenshot-worthy?
- Does it spark conversation/debate?

**Why Only 4% Weight:**

Viral spread is nice, but if it doesn't drive business (Category 9), it's just entertainment.

**Example: High Virality, Low Business Value:**

CC: "Cats are better than dogs"

- Super shareable
- Sparks debate
- Gets attention
- Drives zero business

Fine-tuning is weighted lowest because if you nail the big three, these usually fall into place.

## COMMON SCORE PATTERNS & WHAT THEY MEAN

### Pattern 1: "High Articulation, Low Business Logic"

**Scores:**

- Categories 1-3: 25+/28 (Excellent articulation)
- Category 4: 5/12 (Weak USP connection)
- Category 9: 4/10 (Doesn't drive business)
- **Total: 72/100**

**What This Means:** You have a brilliantly articulated Core Concept that will make your competitors rich.

**Example:** CC: "AI is the future of marketing" USP: "We do Facebook ad management"

Problem: They believe your CC → buy AI software → you get nothing

**Fix:** Adjust CC to necessitate your specific approach, not just the category.

---

### Pattern 2: "Right Idea, Wrong Words"

**Scores:**

- Categories 1-2: 12/20 (Poor articulation)
- Category 4: 11/12 (Strong USP connection)
- Category 9: 9/10 (Drives business)
- **Total: 77/100**

**What This Means:** You've identified the right Core Concept but haven't articulated it optimally.

**Fix:** Generate 20+ articulation variations, score each, find the winner. This is the easiest pattern to fix.

**Time to Fix:** 2-3 hours

### Pattern 3: "Clever But Useless"

**Scores:**

- Categories 2, 10, 12: High (Memorable, emotional, viral)
- Categories 4, 9: Low (No USP connection, no demand creation)
- **Total: 68/100**

**What This Means:** You've created marketing content, not a business driver.

**Example:** CC: "Marketing is dead"

Problem:

- Super shareable
- Gets attention
- Creates no demand for anything specific
- Pure shock value

**Fix:** Rebuild from business logic first, not cleverness.

---

### Pattern 4: "The Goldilocks Problem"

**Scores:**

- All categories: 6-7 out of max
- Nothing spectacular, nothing terrible
- **Total: 75/100**

**What This Means:** You're playing it safe. No edges. No strong positioning.

**Fix:** Make it more extreme. Either simpler or more sophisticated. Either broader or more specific. The middle is where Core Concepts go to die.

## THE REFINEMENT PROCESS

### When Your Score is 70-79

**Step 1: Identify the Gap (15 minutes)**

Look at your lowest-scoring categories:

- If Category 4 (USP Connection) is lowest → business logic issue
- If Categories 1-2 (Articulation) is lowest → execution issue
- If Category 9 (Business Driver) is lowest → strategic issue

**Step 2: Focus Your Effort**

Don't try to fix everything. Fix the highest-weighted weakness first.

**Priority Order:**

1. Category 4 (USP Connection) - 12% weight
2. Category 9 (Business Driver) - 10% weight
3. Categories 1-2 (Articulation) - 20% combined
4. Everything else

**Step 3: Make Specific Changes**

**If Category 4 is weak:**

- Map the logical chain: CC → Implication → Requirement → USP
- Find the break: Where could they go elsewhere?
- Options:
    - Adjust CC to include your unique approach
    - Adjust USP to match CC implications
    - Add bridge concept to tighten connection

**If Categories 1-2 are weak:**

- Go back to Lesson 3 tools
- Generate 20 new articulations
- Use linguistic devices (rhyme, rhythm, alliteration)
- Test each with Stranger Test

**If Category 9 is weak:**

- Test CC with real prospects
- Ask: "If you believed this, what would you do?"
- If they don't say "work with you," adjust CC

**Step 4: Retest**

Run new version through scorecard.

Target: 80+ before proceeding.

**Step 5: Iterate**

Expect 2-4 rounds of refinement for complex positioning.

This is time well spent vs. building chain to wrong destination.

## THE TESTING PROTOCOLS

### Beyond the Scorecard: Real-World Validation

The scorecard gives you objective evaluation, but nothing beats real prospect feedback.

### Test 1: The Stranger Test

**Setup:**

- Find someone unfamiliar with your business
- Explain your Core Concept
- Do NOT explain what you do
- Ask: "Based on that, what do you think the solution is?"

**Success Criteria:**

- They describe your USP or very close to it
- They use your category language
- They don't suggest competitor approaches

**What It Tests:** Category 4 (USP Connection)

---

### Test 2: The 24-Hour Repeat Test

**Setup:**

- Tell someone your Core Concept
- 24 hours later, ask them to repeat it back

**Success Criteria:**

- Verbatim or very close (9-10/10)
- General idea correct (7-8/10)
- Can't recall (below 5/10)

**What It Tests:** Category 2 (Memorability)

---

### Test 3: The Business Test (Most Important)

**Setup:**

- Share Core Concept with 5 actual prospects
- Track their response

**Success Criteria:**

- Request next step (high score)
- Ask about pricing (high score)
- Want to schedule call (high score)
- Compare to competitors (medium score)
- Say "interesting" and nothing else (low score)

**What It Tests:** Category 9 (Business Driver)

---

### Test 4: The Articulation Stress Test

**Setup:**

- Try explaining CC in noisy environment
- Try explaining over phone (no visuals)
- Try writing it in email subject line
- Try fitting it in social media post

**Success Criteria:**

- Works in all mediums = high score
- Needs visuals/time = medium score
- Only works in perfect conditions = low score

**What It Tests:** Categories 1-2 (Simplicity/Memorability)
