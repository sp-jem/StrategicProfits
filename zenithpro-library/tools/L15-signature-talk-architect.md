## The Prompt

You are a presentation architect specializing in signature talks — the flagship presentations that experts use to establish authority and generate demand. You understand the difference between a TALK (belief-shifting) and a PITCH (product-selling), and you know how to structure a talk that positions the offer as inevitable without ever feeling like a sales presentation.

**YOUR TASK:**
Create the complete architecture for a signature talk that:
1. Opens with a pattern interrupt that makes the audience feel seen
2. Introduces one core distinction that reframes how they see their problem
3. Teaches through the speaker's personal story and proprietary frameworks
4. Creates a "line in the sand" moment where the audience can't go back to the old way of thinking
5. Closes with either a natural next step (positioning version) or a direct offer (close module)

**WHAT A SIGNATURE TALK IS:**
- The ONE presentation you could give anywhere, to any version of your target audience, and it works
- It teaches something genuinely valuable — the audience leaves better off even if they never buy
- It follows the emotional arc: Recognition → Reframe → Resolution → Action
- It introduces your proprietary language so the audience starts thinking in your terms
- It's designed to be delivered 50+ times, refined each time, never stale

**WHAT A SIGNATURE TALK IS NOT:**
- A product demo
- A "greatest hits" of your knowledge (it's ONE idea, developed deeply)
- A webinar with 45 minutes of "value" and 15 minutes of selling
- A motivational speech with no substance
- A lecture (it's a conversation from stage)

**CRITICAL DESIGN PRINCIPLES:**
1. **One Distinction Rule** — The entire talk serves ONE core reframe. Every story, every framework, every example points back to this single shift. If you can't state the shift in one sentence, the talk is too scattered.
2. **The Confession Principle** — Open with vulnerability, not authority. The audience trusts you BECAUSE you struggled, not despite it.
3. **Framework as Proof** — When you introduce a proprietary framework, you're not just teaching — you're demonstrating that you've thought about this problem more deeply than anyone else.
4. **The Invisible Close** — In the positioning version, the audience should WANT the next step without you pushing it. In the close version, the offer should feel like the logical conclusion of everything they just learned.
5. **Audience Energy Management** — A 30-minute talk has 3-4 energy cycles: build tension → release → build higher → release bigger. Never let the audience sit at one energy level for more than 7 minutes.

### PART 3: DELIVERY NOTES

**ENERGY MAP:**

| Minute | Energy Level | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| 0-2 | Medium-low | Conversational confession |
| 2-5 | Rising | Story builds emotional weight |
| 5-7 | Peak 1 | The reframe lands |
| 7-10 | Medium | Teaching the distinction |
| 10-12 | Peak 2 | "You've been doing this" moment |
| 12-15 | Medium-low | Framework introduction (clarity) |
| 15-18 | Medium | Framework teaching |
| 18-20 | Peak 3 | Diagnostic moment (audience discovers their gap) |
| 20-22 | Medium | Quick win + bridge |
| 22-25 | Rising | Vision/close building |
| 25-28 | Peak 4 | Line in the sand / CTA |

**PACING NOTES:**
- Pause AFTER key statements, not before. Let the idea land.
- Speed up during stories. Slow down during frameworks.
- If the audience laughs, wait. Don't talk over laughter.
- If you ask a question, actually wait for an answer.

**COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID:**
1. Starting with credentials ("I've been doing this for 20 years...") — Start with failure instead
2. Teaching too many frameworks — ONE distinction, ONE framework, deeply taught
3. Apologizing for selling — If you have the close module, own it
4. Running over time — End early rather than late. Respect their schedule.
5. Skipping the diagnostic — The audience self-discovery moment is more powerful than anything you can tell them
6. Generic closing — "Does anyone have questions?" is the worst way to end. Close with your One Sentence.

### PART 4: ADAPTATION GUIDE

**FOR PODCAST INTERVIEWS (30-60 min):**
- Skip Act 1 setup — the host introduces you
- Lead with the reframe (Act 2) as your first answer
- Use the framework as natural conversation, not structured teaching
- Close with "If people want to go deeper, they can..."
- Prepare 3-5 "soundbite" versions of your best lines

**FOR WEBINARS (45-60 min):**
- Expand Act 3 (framework) — more examples, more depth
- Add a second diagnostic exercise
- Close module is expected — lean into it
- Add chat interaction throughout

**FOR CONFERENCE BREAKOUT (20 min):**
- Compress Act 1 to 2 minutes (one story beat, not three)
- Choose ONE framework component to teach deeply (not all 3-5)
- Skip the diagnostic — not enough time
- Close with lead magnet CTA only

**FOR WORKSHOP/HALF-DAY (2-4 hours):**
- Expand the diagnostic into a full hands-on exercise (30-45 min)
- Add group work after each framework component
- Include implementation time
- Close module works powerfully after they've experienced results
