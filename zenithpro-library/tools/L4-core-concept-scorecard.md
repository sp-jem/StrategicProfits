
## Comprehensive Evaluation System for Core Concept Excellence

### PURPOSE

This tool provides a rigorous, multi-dimensional scoring system to evaluate whether your Core Concept is optimally articulated and logically necessitates your USP. Unlike subjective "gut feel" evaluations, this scorecard uses 12 weighted categories with specific sub-questions to generate an objective quality score.

**Strategic Value:** A Core Concept is more foundational than a mechanism - it's the paradigm shift that makes your entire category inevitable. This scorecard ensures you're not building a belief chain around a weak foundation.

**Scoring System:** Each category is scored 1-5 (or specific sub-scores), weighted by importance, generating a final score out of 100. Minimum threshold: 80 for proceeding to belief chain construction.

1. **Articulation Simplicity** (Weight: 10%)
2. **Memorability & Repeatability** (Weight: 10%)

## CATEGORY 1: ARTICULATION SIMPLICITY SCORE (10 points)

**Core Principle:** The Mark Ford Rule - "It's not just about the idea. It's about the articulation of the idea."

### Sub-Questions (Rate Each 1-5)

**Q1: Can it be stated in ONE sentence?**

- 5 = Yes, perfectly clear single sentence
- 3 = Needs 2 sentences or qualifying clause
- 1 = Requires paragraph to explain

**Q2: Could a 5th grader understand it?**

- 5 = Elementary school child would grasp it
- 3 = Requires high school level reasoning
- 1 = Needs advanced/technical knowledge

**Q3: Does it use concrete language vs. abstract concepts?**

- 5 = All concrete, tangible words (ads, position, tactics)
- 3 = Mix of concrete and abstract
- 1 = Mostly abstract (synergy, optimization, alignment)

**Q4: Can you explain it in under 15 seconds?**

- 5 = Yes, and prospect immediately gets it
- 3 = 15-30 seconds needed
- 1 = Requires longer setup/explanation

**Q5: Does it require teaching NEW concepts to understand?**

- 5 = No, uses what they already know
- 3 = Requires 1-2 new concepts explained first
- 1 = Requires extensive new framework

### SCORING

- Average the 5 sub-scores
- Multiply by 2 to get score out of 10
- **Target: 8+ out of 10**

### RED FLAGS

❌ Uses jargon or industry terminology  
❌ Requires multiple clauses or qualifiers  
❌ People ask "what do you mean by that?"  
❌ You find yourself explaining it differently each time

## CATEGORY 2: MEMORABILITY & REPEATABILITY SCORE (10 points)

**Core Principle:** If prospects can't remember it or repeat it, it didn't install. Social capital test: would someone WANT to share this?

### Sub-Questions (Rate Each 1-5)

**Q1: Can someone repeat it verbatim after hearing it once?**

- 5 = Yes, exact wording sticks
- 3 = They get the gist but not exact words
- 1 = They struggle to recall even the concept

**Q2: Does it use memorable linguistic devices?**

- 5 = Rhyme, alliteration, parallel structure, or rhythm
    - _Example: "Your ads aren't broken. Your position's unspoken."_
- 3 = Some pattern but not strong
- 1 = No linguistic devices

**Q3: Is it "sticky" - does it lodge in your mind?**

- 5 = You find yourself thinking about it hours later
- 3 = Somewhat memorable but fades
- 1 = Forgettable immediately

**Q4: Does sharing it make the sharer look smart/informed?**

- 5 = High social capital - impressive to share
- 3 = Neutral - doesn't hurt or help status
- 1 = Makes sharer look confused or trying too hard

**Q5: Would they naturally reference it in conversation?**

- 5 = Perfect cocktail party share
- 3 = Maybe if topic comes up
- 1 = Wouldn't naturally share

### SCORING

- Average the 5 sub-scores
- Multiply by 2 to get score out of 10
- **Target: 8+ out of 10**

### TESTING METHOD

**The 24-Hour Test:**

- Tell someone your Core Concept
- Ask them to repeat it back 24 hours later
- Score based on accuracy of recall

## CATEGORY 4: USP CONNECTION LOGIC (12 points) ⭐ HIGHEST WEIGHT

**Core Principle:** The business-critical test. Does believing the CC NECESSITATE your specific solution, or could they believe it and buy from a competitor?

### Sub-Questions (Rate Each 1-5)

**Q1: If someone fully accepts the CC, must they logically need YOUR solution?**

- 5 = CC makes your USP the only logical conclusion
- 3 = CC suggests need for category, but not specifically you
- 2 = CC creates interest but many solution paths possible
- 1 = No clear connection between CC and your solution

**Q2: The Competitive Vulnerability Test: "They believe my CC but still might..."**

- 5 = "...have no choice but to work with me specifically"
- 3 = "...need this category of solution but could choose competitors"
- 1 = "...solve it completely differently than my approach"

**Q3: Does the CC explain WHY your approach (not just category) is required?**

- 5 = CC logically necessitates your unique methodology
    - _Example: "Positioning requires both strategy AND implementation coaching - pure consulting leaves you stuck, pure courses overwhelm - done-with-you solves both"_
- 3 = CC points to category but not your differentiation
- 1 = CC is generic, could apply to anyone

**Q4: Can you complete this sentence: "Once they believe [CC], they MUST..."**

- 5 = "...engage my specific approach because [logical necessity]"
- 3 = "...need this type of solution but could choose method"
- 1 = Sentence doesn't complete logically

**Q5: Does your USP deliver the ONLY way to fulfill the CC's implication?**

- 5 = You're the logical monopoly given the CC
- 3 = You're one good option among several
- 1 = USP is disconnected from CC implications

**Q6: Gap Test: What could someone believe CC and still NOT buy from you?**

- 5 = Nothing - CC closes all escape routes to your USP
- 3 = They might try DIY or adjacent categories first
- 1 = Many alternatives seem equally logical

### SCORING

- Average the 6 sub-scores
- Multiply by 2.4 to get score out of 12
- **Target: 10+ out of 12** (This is CRITICAL)

### THE FATAL FLAW CHECK

❌ **"Great CC, Wrong Business" Problem:**  
If you score 9+ on articulation but 6 or below here, you have a brilliant Core Concept that grows someone else's business.

**Example of Fatal Flaw:**

- CC: "Marketing automation is the future"
- USP: "We do Facebook ads"
- Problem: CC creates demand for automation software, not your service

## USING THE SCORECARD

### STEP 1: GATHER INPUTS

**You Need:**

- Your Core Concept (final articulation from Lesson 3)
- 3-5 alternative CC articulations
- Your USP statement
- Your unique methodology description
- List of competitor solution types
- Top 3 past solutions prospects have tried

### STEP 2: SCORE EACH CATEGORY

For each of the 12 categories:

1. Answer each sub-question honestly (1-5 scale)
2. Calculate category average
3. Apply multiplier to get category score
4. Record score out of max possible

**Be Brutally Honest:**

- Don't "cheat" yourself by inflating scores
- Ask trusted advisors to score independently
- Test with actual prospects when possible

### STEP 3: CALCULATE WEIGHTED TOTAL

- Multiply each category score by its weight
- Sum all weighted scores
- Result = your final CC quality score out of 100

### STEP 4: IDENTIFY WEAK LINKS

- Any category scoring below 60% of max is a red flag
- Example: Category 4 scores 7/12 = 58% → CRITICAL ISSUE
- Focus refinement on lowest-scoring high-weight categories

### STEP 5: REFINE & RETEST

**If Score < 80:**

1. **Articulation Issues (Categories 1-3)?**
    
    - Go back to Lesson 3 tools
    - Generate 20+ new variations
    - Test each through scorecard
    - Pick highest scorer
2. **USP Connection Issues (Category 4)?**
    
    - Map logical chain explicitly
    - Identify gaps/leaps
    - Options:
        - Strengthen CC to require your approach
        - Adjust USP to match CC implications
        - Add bridge concept between CC and USP
3. **Category Creation Issues (Category 5)?**
    
    - Make paradigm shift more radical
    - Ensure old category feels obsolete
    - Name the new category explicitly
4. **Business Logic Issues (Category 9)?**
    
    - Test with prospects: "If you believed X, would you need Y?"
    - If CC doesn't drive purchase, it's wrong CC
5. **Iterate Until Score ≥ 80**

## VALIDATION TESTING METHODS

### Test 1: The Stranger Test

- Explain CC to someone unfamiliar with your business
- Do NOT explain what you do
- Ask: "What do you think the solution is?"
- If they describe your USP → strong connection
- If they suggest something else → weak connection

### Test 2: The Repeat Test

- Tell someone your CC
- 24 hours later, ask them to repeat it
- Score accuracy:
    - Verbatim = 5
    - Close paraphrase = 4
    - General idea = 3
    - Wrong = 1

### Test 3: The Competitor Test

- Show CC to prospect
- Ask: "Who could help you with this?"
- If they say "you" or "someone like you" = category created
- If they say your name = tight USP connection
- If they name competitor or wrong category = weak

### Test 4: The Business Test (Most Important)

- Show CC to 5 actual prospects
- Track:
    - Do they request next step?
    - Do they ask about pricing?
    - Do they mention competitors?
    - Do they seem confused?
- High engagement + low competitor mentions = strong

## COMMON FAILURE PATTERNS

### PATTERN 1: "Clever But Disconnected"

**Symptoms:**

- High scores on Categories 1-3, 10, 12 (articulation, emotion, viral)
- Low scores on Category 4, 9 (USP connection, business driver)

**Problem:** Brilliant CC that makes your competitor rich

**Fix:** Rebuild CC to necessitate your unique approach, not just category

---

### PATTERN 2: "Generic Positioning"

**Symptoms:**

- Medium scores across most categories
- Low score on Category 5 (category creation)
- High score on Category 6 reversed (competitors could claim it)

**Problem:** Better positioning in existing category vs. new category

**Fix:** Make paradigm shift more radical, name new category

---

### PATTERN 3: "Complicated Clever"

**Symptoms:**

- Low scores on Categories 1-2 (simplicity, memorability)
- High sophistication (Category 11)

**Problem:** Too smart for its own good

**Fix:** Simplify language, test with outsiders

---

### PATTERN 4: "Past-Focused Only"

**Symptoms:**

- High score on Category 7 (explains failures)
- Low score on Category 9 (business driver)

**Problem:** Great at explaining past, but doesn't drive future purchase

**Fix:** Ensure CC not just diagnostic but prescriptive for your solution

## IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

### This Scorecard Is:

- More comprehensive than mechanism scorecard (12 vs 11 categories)
- Heavily weighted toward business logic (Categories 4, 9)
- Designed to catch "clever but useless" Core Concepts
- Iterative - expect 2-4 rounds of refinement

### This Scorecard Is NOT:

- One-and-done evaluation
- Subjective "gut feel" assessment
- Focused on creativity over business results
- Lenient - 80+ is genuinely high bar

### Success Pattern:

1. Generate 12+ CC articulations (Lesson 3)
2. Score each through this scorecard
3. Identify winner + weak spots
4. Refine weak spots
5. Retest
