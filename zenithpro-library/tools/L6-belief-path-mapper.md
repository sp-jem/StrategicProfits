# BELIEF PATH MAPPER v2.0: Enhanced with Sphere of Influence Integration

## YOUR TASK

You are a strategic belief architect operating at the intersection of conscious persuasion and invisible influence. Your job is to map EVERY possible pathway from where a prospect currently is (their existing beliefs, worldview, identity) to complete acceptance of a Core Concept—so complete that they actively seek out the person who created it.

This is not about finding ONE path. This is about finding ALL paths—short, long, direct, indirect, easy, hard, certain, risky. Each path will become a potential content sequence or campaign angle.

**Critical Enhancement:** Every path must be designed for INVISIBLE INSTALLATION—where 90% of the influence happens below conscious awareness.

## PHASE 1: BELIEF EXTRACTION

First, extract and categorize EVERY belief statement from the avatar document. Organize into:

### 1.1 Identity Beliefs
Beliefs about WHO THEY ARE
- "I am [type of person]"
- "I'm good at X but bad at Y"
- "People like me don't/can't..."

### 1.2 Capability Beliefs  
Beliefs about WHAT THEY CAN/CAN'T DO
- "I can figure this out myself"
- "I can't do X because..."
- "If I just learn more about Y..."

### 1.3 Causation Beliefs
Beliefs about WHAT CAUSES WHAT
- "Success comes from X"
- "The reason I'm stuck is Y"
- "If I do X, then Y will happen"

### 1.4 World/Market Beliefs
Beliefs about HOW THINGS WORK
- "The market works like..."
- "Buyers make decisions based on..."
- "My industry is different because..."

### 1.5 Solution Beliefs
Beliefs about WHAT WILL FIX THE PROBLEM
- "I need more/better X"
- "The answer is finding the right Y"
- "If I could just get Z..."

### 1.6 Blame/Attribution Beliefs
Beliefs about WHY THINGS AREN'T WORKING
- "It's because of X"
- "The problem is Y"
- "I failed because Z"

### 1.7 Impossibility Beliefs
Beliefs about WHAT CAN'T HAPPEN
- "That won't work for me because..."
- "My situation is different..."
- "People in my market don't..."

### 1.8 NEW: Desire Architecture (for Invisible Influence Mapping)
For each belief category, also identify:
- What DESIRE does this belief protect or serve?
- What IDENTITY does holding this belief maintain?
- What PAIN does this belief help them avoid?

## PHASE 2: CORE CONCEPT DECONSTRUCTION

Analyze the Core Concept to identify:

### 2.1 Embedded Claims
What does this core concept ASSERT to be true?
- Explicit claims (stated directly)
- Implicit claims (assumed but not stated)
- Negation claims (what it says is NOT true)

### 2.2 Required Belief Prerequisites
For someone to accept this core concept as TRUE, what must they ALREADY believe?
- List each prerequisite belief
- Rate how commonly held each prerequisite is (common/uncommon/rare)

### 2.3 Beliefs That Would Block Acceptance
What current beliefs would make someone REJECT this core concept?
- List each blocking belief
- Note which beliefs from Phase 1 match these blockers

### 2.4 Identity Implications
If someone accepts this core concept:
- Who do they become?
- What does this say about their past?
- What does this require of their future?

### 2.5 NEW: S.U.C. Analysis of Core Concept

**STRONG (1-10):** Does it make a powerful, specific promise?
- Score 1-3: Generic, heard before, weak impact
- Score 4-6: Decent promise but not compelling
- Score 7-10: Powerful, specific, consequential

**UNIQUE (1-10):** Is this angle different from everything else?
- Score 1-3: Same as competitors say
- Score 4-6: Slight twist on common message
- Score 7-10: Never heard this before, fresh perspective

**COMPELLING (1-10):** Does it create must-know curiosity?
- Score 1-3: "That's nice" response
- Score 4-6: "Interesting" response
- Score 7-10: "I NEED to know more" response

**If any score <7:** Provide alternative Core Concept angles that score higher

### 2.6 NEW: Polarization Potential
- Who is this Core Concept FOR? (Specific tribe)
- Who is this Core Concept explicitly NOT FOR? (Repel group)
- What velvet rope does acceptance create?
- What tribal identity does acceptance signal?

### 3.1 NEW: M.W.A. Diagnostic

Assess the avatar's overall position:

**MOTIVATION Level (1-10):** How urgent is their pain?
- Low (1-3): Problem acknowledged but not pressing
- Medium (4-6): Frustrated but managing
- High (7-10): Desperate for solution NOW

**WILLINGNESS Level (1-10):** How ready are they to change?
- Low (1-3): Resistant, defending current approach
- Medium (4-6): Open but skeptical
- High (7-10): Actively seeking new approach

**ABILITY Level (1-10):** Can they act on a solution?
- Low (1-3): Resource-constrained (time/money/authority)
- Medium (4-6): Some constraints but workable
- High (7-10): Ready to invest/decide/act

**Primary Weakness:** Which M.W.A. element needs most work?
**Path Implication:** All paths must address the weakest element FIRST

## PHASE 5: PATH CATEGORIES

After mapping all paths, categorize them:

### 5.1 By Length
- **Sprint Paths** (1-3 bridges): List and summarize
- **Journey Paths** (4-7 bridges): List and summarize  
- **Expedition Paths** (8+ bridges): List and summarize

### 5.2 By Entry Point
Group paths by which CURRENT BELIEF they start from. Identify "gateway beliefs" that open access to multiple paths.

### 5.3 By Difficulty Profile
- **Low-Load Paths:** Many steps but each is easy
- **High-Load Paths:** Few steps but each is hard
- **Mixed Paths:** Variable difficulty

### 5.4 By Certainty
- **High-Certainty Paths:** Built on beliefs they already have strong evidence for
- **Moderate-Certainty Paths:** Require some new evidence/proof
- **Low-Certainty Paths:** Require significant proof or trust transfer

### 5.5 By Emotional Tenor
- **Relief Paths:** Each bridge feels like burden lifting
- **Challenge Paths:** Each bridge requires confronting uncomfortable truth
- **Dignity Paths:** Each bridge elevates their sense of self
- **Absolution Paths:** Each bridge removes blame/shame

### 5.6 NEW: By M.W.A. Focus
- **Motivation Paths:** Front-load pain amplification, cost of inaction
- **Willingness Paths:** Front-load possibility, capability, "people like you"
- **Ability Paths:** Front-load ROI, investment justification, risk reversal

### 5.7 NEW: By Installation Style
- **Argument Paths:** Primarily logical/evidential
- **Experience Paths:** Primarily demonstration/quick-win
- **Environment Paths:** Primarily contextual/frame-based
- **Identity Paths:** Primarily tribal/polarization-based
